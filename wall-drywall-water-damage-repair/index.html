Wall and Drywall Water Damage in Mount Vernon, OH

Wet drywall, baseboards and wall cavities, and when walls can be dried instead of replaced.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

Wall and drywall water damage repair in Mount Vernon, OH starts with finding out how wet the wall is inside, because the surface can look fine while the cavity is soaked. If a wall is stained, soft or bubbling, turn off the source, keep the area ventilated and call us before painting or patching.

Drywall is paper and gypsum, and it absorbs water fast. A wall that is wet for more than a day or two may need to be cut out, and one caught early can often be dried in place. If the water came from the ceiling above, the ceiling page explains that path.

Signs of water damage in a wall

  • Paint bubbles, peeling or brown stains on the surface.
  • A soft or crumbly feel when you press the drywall.
  • Baseboards that swell, warp or separate from the wall.
  • A musty smell in the room that gets worse when it is humid.
  • Cold, damp spots detected by a thermal camera behind the surface.

What happens inside the wall

Water runs down inside the wall cavity and collects at the bottom plate. It wets insulation, which holds water like a sponge, and it wets framing, which dries slowly. That is why a stain near the floor can point to a leak much higher up.

The crew uses a moisture meter and thermal camera to map the wet area, then drills small test holes or opens the drywall to see inside.

Dry the wall or cut it out

Drywall that is only damp and intact can sometimes be dried with air movers and a dehumidifier, with small ventilation openings at the baseboard. Drywall that is soft, stained, or wet with contaminated water is cut out, along with wet insulation.

Cutting usually goes about a foot or two above the visible line. That removes water that wicked upward and lets the framing dry. The structural drying page explains the equipment.

Baseboards, plaster and old walls

Older Mount Vernon homes may have plaster and lath instead of drywall, especially in neighborhoods near the East High Street Historic District. Plaster dries differently and can hold water longer, so the crew tests it before deciding.

Baseboards are removed so the wall bottom can dry, and often reinstalled once readings are dry. Water damage on baseboards is one of the earliest signs of a leak.

Repair after drying

Once readings are dry, new insulation and drywall are installed, taped, textured and painted. A stain-blocking primer helps on small stains on dry drywall.

Painting before the wall is dry traps moisture and lets mold grow, so the crew confirms readings first. If growth is found, the mold remediation page describes the steps.

Insulation, studs and what stays

Fiberglass insulation that was wet usually comes out, because it sags and holds moisture against the studs. Wood studs and plates can be dried in place and checked with a meter, and they normally stay.

If framing stays wet for a long period, it can need treatment or replacement, and the crew will show you the readings. The aim is to leave sound framing in place and remove only what cannot dry.

What affects the cost of wall and drywall water damage

Wall repairs range from a dried panel to a rebuilt room.

  • Wall area wet. More wet wall means more drywall, insulation and drying.
  • Water category. Contaminated water means removal, not drying.
  • Wall material. Drywall and plaster each repair differently.
  • Insulation. Wet insulation is replaced.
  • Finish work. Taping, texture, primer and paint are part of the rebuild.

Wall damage and insurance

Wall damage from a sudden leak is often covered, while damage from slow, long-term moisture often is not. Ask your insurer, and photograph the wall before it is opened.

Questions about wall and drywall water damage in Mount Vernon

Can I just paint over the stain?

Only after the wall and cavity read dry and the source is fixed. Paint over a wet wall traps moisture.

How much drywall will be cut out?

The crew removes what is wet, often a foot or two above the visible line, and shows readings so you can see the reason.

Will baseboards need replacing?

Often they can be removed, dried and reinstalled, but swollen baseboards are replaced.

Should I remove the baseboard myself?

You can, carefully, but let the crew decide how far to open the wall. They know where the wet area ends and how to keep the trim for reuse.
