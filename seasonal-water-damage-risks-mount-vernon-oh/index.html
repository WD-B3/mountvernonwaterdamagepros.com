Seasonal Water Damage Risks to Check Every Year

A season-by-season list of the leaks and drainage problems worth checking before they cost you.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Mount Vernon Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Water damage is not random, and it follows the calendar. Spring thaws and storms, summer humidity, fall leaves and winter freezes each stress a different part of the house, so a short check once a season catches most problems early.

Here is a seasonal checklist for Mount Vernon, OH homes. It is a prevention guide, and each item takes minutes.

Spring: thaw, rain and runoff

  • Check the roof and attic for stains after the first heavy rains.
  • Clear gutters and test the sump pump before the wet months.
  • Look at the foundation for new cracks or seepage.
  • Check outdoor spigots and hoses for damage from freezing.

Summer: humidity and appliances

  • Have the AC condensate line checked, since it clogs when cooling runs hard.
  • Inspect washing machine hoses, dishwasher lines and the water heater for drips.
  • Watch humidity in basements, and run a dehumidifier if the air feels damp.
  • Look at the caulking around tubs and showers.

Fall: leaves and preparation

  • Clean gutters again after the leaves fall.
  • Disconnect and drain outdoor hoses, and shut off outdoor spigots if you can.
  • Insulate pipes in garages, crawl spaces and exterior walls.
  • Have the heating system serviced, since a failed furnace in a freeze puts pipes at risk.

Winter: freeze and thaw

  • Keep the heat on, even if you are away, and open cabinet doors on exterior walls during a hard freeze.
  • Let a faucet drip when the forecast is very cold.
  • Know where the main shutoff is.
  • Watch for ice dams and stains on upstairs ceilings.

A pipe that froze may leak only when it thaws, so a house can be fine overnight and wet by noon. The frozen pipe page explains what happens next.

Year-round habits

Know your shutoffs, keep an eye on your water bill and fix drips early. A leak sensor in the basement, under the kitchen sink and by the water heater costs little and gives early warning.

If you find damp spots between seasons, dry them fast. The structural drying page explains how a crew confirms a structure is dry.

Aging plumbing and appliances

Rubber hoses on washers, dishwashers and ice makers age and crack, and many manufacturers suggest replacing them on a schedule. Check your manual, and look for bulges, cracks and rust on fittings.

Water heaters, supply lines and shutoff valves also wear out. A plumber can tell you what to replace before it fails, and a few replacements cost less than a flooded floor.

Away from home: a short checklist

  • Shut off the main water valve if you will be gone for days.
  • Leave the heat on in winter, set to a safe level.
  • Ask a neighbor to check the house.
  • Know whether your policy has conditions for vacant periods.

A leak that runs while no one is home does the most damage, since no one stops it.

Mount Vernon note: spring has a history here

The 1913 flood began with heavy rain after winter snowmelt had filled Ohio rivers, and Knox County communities along the Kokosing, such as Zuck, were so badly damaged that residents chose not to rebuild, according to local newspaper reporting. The lesson for a homeowner is that thaw, saturated ground and heavy rain can arrive together.

Check the FEMA flood map for your address, ask the city building department what applies to your lot and keep the sump and gutters ready by March. For a Gambier address, the Gambier page covers the village in the same river valley.

Common questions

What is the most common winter water damage problem?

Pipes that freeze and split in unheated spaces, though no local count exists that we can cite. Insulating pipes and keeping heat on helps.

Do I need to check things every season?

A short check each season is enough for most homes. Focus on gutters, shutoffs and any pipes in cold spaces.

How can I tell if a stain is old or new?

A moisture meter can show if it is still wet. A dry stain is old, and a damp one means the source is still active.

How often should I replace washer hoses?

Follow the manufacturer guidance and inspect them yearly. Replace any hose that shows bulges, cracks or rust.
