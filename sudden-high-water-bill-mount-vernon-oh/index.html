A Sudden Jump in Your Water Bill: What It Can Mean

Simple tests for a hidden leak, what to ask the utility and when to call a plumber or a restoration crew.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Mount Vernon Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

A water bill that suddenly jumps, with no change in how many people live in the house or how much water they use, is worth a closer look. The cause may be a hidden leak, and a hidden leak can damage floors, walls and the structure under them.

This guide explains simple ways to test for a leak, what other causes to rule out and what to do if you find water where it should not be.

Rule out the obvious first

  • A guest, a new appliance or a seasonal change such as watering a lawn.
  • A running toilet, which can waste a lot of water without making noise.
  • A dripping faucet or outdoor spigot.
  • A different billing period length, which can make a bill look higher.

The meter test

  1. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water.
  2. Find the water meter and note the reading, or the small dial that moves with even slight flow.
  3. Wait 30 minutes to an hour without using water.
  4. Read the meter again, and if the number moved or the dial turned, water is flowing somewhere.

If the meter moves with everything off, shut off the main valve and see if it stops. If it stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving after the main is closed, call the utility, because the leak may be on the street side.

Toilet and fixture checks

A few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank, with no flushing for fifteen minutes, will show color in the bowl if the flapper leaks. A faucet drip can add up over a month.

These are cheap fixes and often explain a jump. If they do not, the leak may be behind a wall or under a floor.

Signs of a hidden leak

Look for a damp spot on a floor or a wall, a musty smell, warped baseboards, a warm patch on a floor, the sound of running water when everything is off or an unexplained stain on a ceiling.

If your home has a slab foundation or a pipe in a wall, those signs may be the only warning. For slab homes, the slab leak page explains the symptoms and the drying that follows, and for a leak that already wet a wall, see the wall and drywall page.

Who to call

A plumber finds and fixes the leak, and a restoration crew dries what the water reached. If water has soaked a floor or wall, the longer it stays, the more it costs.

Ask your utility how it handles bills that include a documented leak, and keep the plumber invoice and photos. Policies on adjustments vary, so ask before you assume.

Other causes of a high bill

  • Irrigation, filling a pool or a hose left running.
  • A water softener or filter that runs more than it should.
  • A water heater leak that you have not seen.
  • Extra household members or a long stretch of guests.
  • A bill that covers more days than usual.

Reading your bill and meter

Look at the usage units, which may be gallons or hundred cubic feet, and compare them with the same month last year. A single spike points to an event, and a slow climb points to a worsening leak.

Keep each bill, and note meter readings if you test. If you call the utility, those numbers help them check whether the problem is on your side or theirs.

Mount Vernon note: the city utility and your bill

The City of Mount Vernon bills water through its utility, and its website offers an online portal for paying a water bill. If your bill jumped, ask the city utility how it reads your meter and whether it can review the usage, and ask what documentation it wants if you find and repair a leak.

The city water comes from groundwater, so the supply itself is clean at the start of any leak, which helps with drying if you catch it early. In Gambier, the village maintains the distribution lines and buys its water from the city, as the Gambier page explains, so ask the village how bills are handled there.

Common questions

How much can a running toilet add to a bill?

It can add a lot over a month, and it is one of the most common causes of a jump. Check the flapper and the fill valve first.

Will the city adjust my bill after a leak?

That is a question for the city utility, which sets its own policies. Ask what documents it wants, and keep the repair invoice.

Should I call you or a plumber?

Call a plumber to find and repair the leak, and call us if water has soaked floors, walls or ceilings.

How fast can a hidden leak run up a bill?

A small continuous leak can add a lot over a billing period, and a larger leak can add far more. That is why a quick meter test is worth doing.
