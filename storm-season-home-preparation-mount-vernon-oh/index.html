How to Prepare Your Home for Storm Season

Simple steps to take before storm season, from the roofline down to the sump pit.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Mount Vernon Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Storm season is easier on a house that has been checked before the first heavy rain. Most storm water damage starts with something small, such as a clogged gutter, a downspout that dumps beside the foundation or a sump pump that has not run in months.

This is a preparation checklist, not a cleanup guide. It covers what to look at, what to test and what to keep on hand, and it is written for Mount Vernon, OH homeowners.

Start at the roof and gutters

  • Look for missing, lifted or cracked shingles, and check flashing around chimneys and vents.
  • Clear leaves from gutters and make sure downspouts are attached.
  • Check that downspouts discharge several feet from the foundation.
  • Trim branches that overhang the roof, since limbs fall in wind.

Check the foundation and grading

Walk the outside after a rain and look for water pooling against the house. Soil should slope away from the foundation, and window wells should be clear and covered.

Cracks in the foundation that seep in rain are a repair job for a contractor. Documenting them with photos helps you track changes.

Test the sump pump

If you have a sump pump, pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch it start and clear. Check the discharge line and the check valve, and consider a battery backup, since storms often cut power.

A pump that has not run for months can stick, so test before you need it.

Know your shutoffs

Find the main water shutoff and the electrical panel, and make sure everyone in the house knows where they are. In an emergency, shutting off water and power fast can save a lot.

Keep a flashlight, a phone charger and a list of emergency numbers where you can find them.

Move and protect belongings

Store valuables and important papers off the floor in a basement, in sealed plastic bins or on shelves. Keep copies of your insurance policy and photos of your belongings in the cloud or somewhere off site.

Take a home inventory video now. It costs nothing and is useful after any loss.

What to do when the storm hits

Stay safe, stay out of floodwater and call the utility or 911 for downed lines or gas smells. After the storm, photograph damage before cleaning, and call for help if water entered.

If rain came through the roof, the ceiling page explains the interior damage, and the storm water damage page covers cleanup after the event.

A small emergency kit worth keeping

  • A flashlight, extra batteries and a phone charger.
  • Work gloves, rubber boots and heavy trash bags.
  • A tarp, plastic sheeting and duct tape.
  • A list of emergency numbers, including the 24/7 line, the utility and your insurer.
  • A copy of your insurance policy and photos of your belongings.

Insurance steps to take before the season

Read your policy once a year and check what it says about water, wind and flood. Ask your agent about flood coverage and sewer backup endorsements, and note any waiting periods.

Take dated photos of each room and of the outside of the house, and keep them off site. After a loss, those photos make a claim easier.

Mount Vernon note: the county plan and the city drains

According to local news coverage, the Mount Vernon city council adopted the Knox County hazard mitigation plan to keep the city eligible for federal grants, and the county emergency management agency publishes preparedness information. The City of Mount Vernon also runs a stormwater utility to maintain public drainage.

Public drainage helps streets, and your own gutters and downspouts protect your foundation. Look at the FEMA flood map for your lot, and for a Gambier address see the Gambier page. The statewide flood of 1913 began with a long rain on saturated ground, a reminder that wet conditions before a storm matter as much as the storm.

Common questions

How often should I clean gutters?

At least twice a year, and more if you have many trees nearby. Check them after heavy storms.

Is a battery backup for a sump pump worth it?

Storms often cause power loss, so many homeowners consider one. Ask a plumber about your setup.

Where do I find alerts?

Check with the Knox County emergency management agency and the National Weather Service for ways to receive alerts.

When should I start preparing?

Before the wet months begin. A weekend of checks in spring and fall is enough for most homes.
