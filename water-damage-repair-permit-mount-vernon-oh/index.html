Do You Need a Permit to Repair Water Damage?

Which repairs usually trigger a permit, who to ask and who is responsible for pulling one.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Mount Vernon Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Whether you need a permit to repair water damage depends on what is being repaired, and the rule is set by your local building authority, not by a restoration company. Drying a wet floor is usually different from rebuilding a wall or replacing a water heater.

This guide explains the usual pattern and the right way to get a definite answer for your address in Mount Vernon or Gambier, OH.

The general pattern

Cosmetic repairs, such as paint and replacing flooring, often need no permit, while structural, electrical and plumbing work often does. Replacing a few sheets of drywall is commonly treated differently from rebuilding a wall or moving a pipe.

That is a general pattern, not a rule for your town. Permit rules vary, so ask the city building department before you assume.

Work that often involves a permit

  • Electrical repairs, such as replacing wiring or a panel damaged by water.
  • Plumbing work, including new supply lines or drain changes.
  • Structural repairs to joists, subfloor or framing.
  • Water heater replacement.
  • Work on a gas line.

Work that often does not

  • Drying and extraction, which are mitigation and not construction.
  • Replacing carpet or flooring on top of an intact subfloor.
  • Painting and cosmetic finish work.
  • Replacing a section of drywall with no wiring or framing changes.

Again, ask the authority that covers your address, because the same repair may be treated differently from town to town.

Who is responsible for the permit

In most cases the contractor doing the permitted work pulls the permit, but the property owner is ultimately responsible. Ask the contractor in writing who handles it and whether the cost is in the scope.

An unpermitted repair can cause trouble later, for example when you sell the house or file an insurance claim. Keep copies of every permit and inspection.

Emergency mitigation and permits

Emergency steps, such as pumping water and setting drying equipment, are not construction, and they should not wait for a permit. Once the structure is dry and the rebuild is planned, the permit question comes up.

The structural drying page explains the drying stage, and the ceiling page and other repair pages describe the rebuild stage where permits can matter.

How to ask the building office

Call or visit and give the address, describe the damage and the repair you plan, and ask whether a permit is required and who must apply. Write down the name of the person you spoke to, the date and the answer.

If the office says no permit is needed, ask whether the answer applies to the full scope or only part of it. A short follow-up email can put the answer in writing.

Records to keep after permitted work

  • The permit number and the date it was issued.
  • The inspection results and dates.
  • The contractor name and a copy of the scope.
  • Final sign-off, if the office provides one.

These records matter when you sell, refinance or file a later claim.

Mount Vernon note: where to ask

The City of Mount Vernon website lists portals for engineering permits and code enforcement permits, along with the city office on South Main Street. Which one applies to your repair depends on the work, so call the city office and describe it before you hire anyone.

For Gambier addresses, the village of Gambier is the local authority, and the Gambier page explains how the village differs from the city. Properties outside both may be under the county, so confirm which jurisdiction covers your address.

Common questions

Does drying my house need a permit?

Mitigation steps such as extraction and drying are generally not construction work, but confirm with your local building authority if you are unsure.

Who do I ask in Gambier?

Start with the village office. It will say whether the village or another authority covers your property and your repair.

What if my contractor says no permit is needed?

Ask them to confirm it in writing and check with the city or village yourself. It is your property and your responsibility.

Is a permit the same as an inspection?

No. A permit is the approval to do the work, and an inspection is a check that the work meets the code. A permitted job usually includes inspections.
