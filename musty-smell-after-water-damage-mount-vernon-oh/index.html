How to Get Rid of the Musty Smell After Water Damage

Why the smell lingers after a leak, how to track it and which fixes work.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Mount Vernon Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

A musty smell after water damage is a message. It means something in the house is still damp, even if the surface looks and feels dry. Air fresheners and candles do not answer the message.

This guide explains why the smell lingers, how to find its source and which fixes work. If the smell is strong or comes with visible growth, the next step is a professional check.

Why a musty smell lingers

Smell comes from microbial growth and from damp materials that have not dried. Wet carpet pad, wall insulation, subfloor and the back of drywall are common culprits, and they sit where you cannot see them.

Because warm, still, damp air favors growth, a closed room or a basement holds the smell, and humid days make it worse.

How to track the source

  1. Walk through the house with a nose for where the smell is strongest.
  2. Check under sinks, behind appliances, around windows and at the base of walls.
  3. Press on baseboards and drywall at the smell and feel for softness or cool, damp spots.
  4. Look for stains, peeling paint, warped trim and condensation.
  5. Use a moisture meter if you have one, or ask a professional to map the moisture.

A reading above normal, even without visible mold, means moisture is still present.

What actually removes the odor

The only lasting fix is to find the moisture and dry it. That may mean removing wet pad or drywall, running air movers and dehumidifiers and confirming readings are dry. Once the cause is gone, odor usually fades or can be cleaned.

Deodorizing without drying is a temporary mask. The structural drying page explains how a crew measures and dries a structure so the smell does not return.

When the smell points to mold

If you see growth or the smell is strong and persistent, call for a mold check. The mold remediation page explains containment, removal and the S520 standard.

Do not disturb visible growth, and keep the area closed off. Questions about health belong to your doctor.

Small steps that help in the meantime

  • Run a dehumidifier in the affected room and empty it often.
  • Keep doors open for airflow, unless contaminated water or mold is present.
  • Remove damp items such as rugs, boxes and cloth from the area.
  • Run bathroom and kitchen fans.

Prevent the smell from returning

Fix leaks fast, dry wet materials within a day or two and keep humidity in a moderate range. A hygrometer gives you a number, and a dehumidifier in a damp lower level helps after the water source is fixed.

Odors that are not mold

  • Sewer smell from a dry drain trap, which a little water often fixes.
  • A musty dryer vent or washer that is not cleaned.
  • Stale refrigerator drip pans and dishwasher filters.
  • Damp towels or items stored in a closed space.

Rule these out first, because they are cheap to fix and easy to confuse with water damage.

When the odor persists after a month

If you have dried what you can and the smell remains, get a moisture mapping done. Hidden moisture in a wall, under a floor or in insulation is the usual cause.

A professional check can often find the spot without opening everything. Do not paint over it, because the smell will return.

Mount Vernon note: valley homes and damp lower levels

Mount Vernon and Gambier sit in the Kokosing River valley, and homes there can have damp lower levels after long rain. The FEMA flood map lists whether your lot is in a mapped flood area, and the city building department can tell you what applies.

If the smell is strongest in a basement or crawl space, look for a recent leak, a failed sump or a downspout that discharges near the wall. For a Gambier address, the Gambier page covers the village, and for a crawl space the crawl space page explains the under-floor void.

Common questions

Can a dehumidifier alone fix the smell?

It helps if the moisture source is fixed and the materials are dry. If wet materials remain, it only slows the problem.

Is a musty smell always mold?

Not always, but it is a sign of moisture and often of microbial growth. A check with a moisture meter tells you more than the smell does.

Will the smell go away after the carpet dries?

Sometimes. If the pad or subfloor stayed wet, the smell can remain until they are dried or replaced.

Will an air purifier solve it?

It may reduce airborne particles, but it does not dry a wet material or remove a source. Use it as a help, not a fix.
