Water damage restoration

What happens in the first 24 to 72 hours

Water damage restoration in Mount Vernon, OH is the process of removing water, drying the structure, cleaning or removing what was damaged and rebuilding finished surfaces. The industry follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

The first hours decide most of the outcome. Clean water from a supply line is easiest to handle if it is removed fast, because every hour it sits it soaks deeper into drywall, subfloor and insulation. After about a day, mold can start in warm, damp, still air, and after two or three days porous materials may no longer be saveable.

Here is the order a good response follows. First, the source is stopped, which usually means shutting off the main valve. Second, standing water is extracted with pumps and extraction units. Third, wet materials that cannot be dried are removed, and moisture meters and thermal imaging map what is still wet. Fourth, the equipment runs until moisture readings reach the dry standard, and a written log records it.

Call the line before you start cleaning if the loss is larger than a small spill, because crews work faster when walls and floors are untouched and photos are fresh. If you are not sure whether the water is serious, describe it and we will tell you honestly.

Local conditions

Water damage in Mount Vernon, Gambier and Knox County

Mount Vernon is the Knox County seat, built along the Kokosing River, and the river has shaped its history.

The U.S. Geological Survey mapped floods here in a 1961 atlas, and the statewide flood of 1913 hit communities in the county, so look up your lot on the FEMA flood map and ask the city building department what applies to your lot. A home that sits in or near the valley deserves a plan for rising water, while a home on higher ground is more likely to see leaks and storm water.

The city water supply comes from groundwater, drawn from the Riverside Park well field and a well west of the Kokosing River, according to the city water quality report. For a homeowner that means a supply line break starts with clean water, which is the best case for saving materials, as long as it is removed fast.

The city also runs a wastewater collection system of roughly 219 miles of lines and a stormwater utility, so a backup in your drain may be your own line or the main, and the first call differs.

Housing here ranges from older homes near the East High Street Historic District and downtown to newer neighborhoods along the U.S. 36 corridor and near Mount Vernon Nazarene University. Older homes may have galvanized supply lines, plaster walls and basements, while newer homes tend to have drywall, PEX lines and slab or crawl space foundations. The right drying plan depends on which you have.

Gambier, a few miles east, gets its water from the City of Mount Vernon, and the Gambier page covers that village. The service area page lists the neighborhoods and landmarks we work around.

Water damage restoration questions in Mount Vernon

How quickly can a crew get to my home?

The line is answered 24/7 and same-day response is available when crews are available. We do not promise an arrival time, because it depends on weather, distance and call volume, but you will get shutoff and safety steps on the first call.

What should I do before the crew arrives?

Stop the source if you can, usually by closing the main water valve. Turn off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry, lift valuables off the floor, keep people and pets out of contaminated water and take photos.

Is emergency water removal available at night?

Yes. The line is open every hour of every day, and water loss does not follow business hours. Call and describe what is happening.

Does homeowners insurance cover water damage?

Often when it is sudden and accidental, and often not when it is gradual or caused by neglect. Flood damage usually needs separate flood coverage. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Do you serve Gambier and the rest of Knox County?

Yes, we cover Mount Vernon and Gambier, and the service area page has the detail. If you are farther out, call and we will tell you honestly whether a crew can reach you.

How do I know whether it is water damage or just dampness?

Stains, soft drywall, cupped floors, a musty smell and a meter reading above normal are signs of real water damage. If you are unsure, call and describe it, and we will say what to check.
