Water Damage Restoration in Downtown Mount Vernon, OH

Water removal and drying for businesses and buildings in the downtown historic district.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

Downtown Mount Vernon is the center of the city, built around Public Square, where the city's offices are. A large part of it is listed as the Downtown Mount Vernon Historic District, which the National Register of Historic Places added in 2012.

Water damage here is usually a business problem as much as a building problem, so this page focuses on commercial water damage, how a restoration visit is planned around opening hours, and what to document.

What the downtown district covers

The National Register listing names South Main, South Mulberry, South Gay, High, Vine, Gambier and Howard Streets, plus Ohio Avenue and Phillips Drive. It lists 58 contributing buildings, 2 sites and 3 structures.

That is a historic listing, not a map of businesses or homes, and it does not say how many buildings have apartments above shops. We do not claim any of those details.

Floods in downtown history

A photograph in the Ohio Memory collection shows flooded downtown streets in Mount Vernon on January 21, 1959. The collection notes it was part of the statewide flood of January 1959, the most destructive in Ohio since 1913.

The U.S. Geological Survey also published a report titled Floods at Mount Vernon, Ohio in 1961. That history is a reason to know your building's drainage, but it does not tell you how any single building will behave in a storm today.

Common downtown water problems

  • A sprinkler or supply line that fails above a shop or office.
  • A rooftop drain or flat roof that backs up during rain.
  • A basement or lower level that takes on water through floor drains.
  • A restroom or kitchen supply failure in a shared building.
  • A neighbor's leak that comes through a shared wall or ceiling.

These are typical scenarios for older downtown blocks. They are not a record of incidents in a specific building.

Planning work around a business

Dry-out in an occupied building has to be planned. The crew protects inventory and records, sets containment so dust and moisture do not spread, and tells you which rooms can be used and which are off limits.

If a building is shared, tell the dispatcher which units are affected. The source may be in a neighboring space, and the scope may cross walls and floors.

Documentation that helps a business claim

Keep photos of the damage, a list of affected inventory and equipment, and the dry log. Business interruption and property coverage depend on your policy, so ask your insurer what applies and what proof it wants.

If you rent the space, tell the building owner right away and check the lease for who handles repairs. The owner and tenant often have different policies.

Historic buildings and review

Mount Vernon's zoning code includes a Historic Overlay District. If your building is in a historic area, ask the city whether repairs that change the outside of the building need review before work starts. Drying itself usually does not change the exterior.

Questions about water damage in Downtown Mount Vernon

Do you work in occupied downtown buildings?

Yes, the crew can plan work around the business, protect contents and limit the work area. Tell us the opening hours and which rooms are in use.

Is every downtown building in the historic district?

No. The listing covers a defined set of streets and buildings, and a building can be near it without being in it. Ask the city if you need to know.

What if the leak is from the unit above mine?

Document the damage, tell the building owner or manager, and call. The crew can dry your space while the source is dealt with.

Can drying damage plaster or historic finishes?

Careless drying can, so tell the crew about the finishes and ask how they will protect them. Moisture readings guide how fast the drying goes.
