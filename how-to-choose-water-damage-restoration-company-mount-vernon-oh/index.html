How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company

A buyer guide: what to ask before you sign, what proof to expect and what to avoid.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Mount Vernon Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

When water is in your house, you will probably call the first company that answers, and that is understandable. If you have a few minutes, a handful of questions can separate a careful company from one that is just fast.

Here is a buyer guide for Mount Vernon, OH. It does not recommend any company, including this one, and it is built so you can judge any company by the answers it gives.

Questions to ask before you agree

  • Are your crews licensed and insured, and can you provide proof?
  • Do your crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage?
  • Will I get a written scope and price before work starts?
  • How do you document moisture readings and drying progress?
  • Who does the rebuild, and is it a separate contract?

Records you should expect

A good company gives you a written scope, a moisture map and a drying log with dated readings, plus photos. Those records are not extras, because your insurer and any future buyer will ask for them.

If a company will not provide them, ask why. A job that cannot be documented is hard to defend.

Credentials and what to be careful about

A company may mention certifications. Ask what they cover, who holds them and whether they apply to the crew on your job. A logo on a van is not proof, and proof of insurance should come from the insurer.

Be cautious about any company that promises your claim will be approved, offers to bill your insurer without discussing it or pushes you to sign a form that assigns your benefits right away.

Speed and the 24/7 promise

A 24/7 line is useful, and a promised arrival time is a red flag, because no company controls weather, traffic or how many calls came in. Ask what the first call will include, such as shutoff steps and what to do while waiting.

Ask how quickly the company can extract and start drying. For a typical loss, water extraction comes first, and equipment follows.

Warning signs

  • A lump-sum price with no breakdown.
  • Pressure to sign immediately.
  • No written scope or readings.
  • A request for full payment before work starts.
  • A name that suggests a local company when the crew is not local.

Compare more than price

The cheapest estimate may leave out equipment days, disposal or drying checks. Compare scopes line by line, and ask your insurer whether it has preferred steps. For a loss that started in a pipe, the burst pipe page shows what a good scope should cover.

A script for the first phone call

  • Say what happened, where the water is and whether it is still flowing.
  • Ask what to do right now, such as shutting off the main.
  • Ask what records you will receive and when.
  • Ask who will come, and how the scope and price are explained.
  • Ask what the company does not handle.

After you hire: how to stay informed

Ask for the moisture readings at each visit, and keep a copy of the scope and any changes. Check that equipment is running and that the crew shows up as arranged.

If something seems off, say so right away in writing, and ask your insurer if you need a second opinion. A good company welcomes questions and answers them plainly.

Mount Vernon note: use the state toolkit

The Ohio Department of Insurance publishes a severe weather preparation and recovery toolkit that includes guidance on recognizing and avoiding contractor fraud with property repairs, and its consumer services office takes questions and complaints. It is listed at insurance.ohio.gov.

For homes in Gambier, the same checks apply, and the Gambier page covers the village. Ask any company where its crews are based and who will be at your house.

Common questions

Should I choose the closest company?

Distance can affect how fast a crew arrives, but it is only one factor. Ask about records, standards and scope as well.

Is it okay to get a second estimate in an emergency?

Yes, but do not delay extraction while you wait. A second opinion is better made after the water is out.

Does my insurer have to approve my contractor?

Usually you can choose your contractor, but your policy may have conditions. Ask your insurer.

How do I check that someone is licensed and insured?

Ask for proof of insurance, which should come from the insurer, and ask what licenses apply to the work. Check them before you sign.
