Kitchen Water Damage in Mount Vernon, OH

Swollen cabinets, leaks under the sink and wet floors, from the cabinet base to the subfloor.

Kitchen water damage restoration in Mount Vernon, OH covers the cabinets, sink area, dishwasher space and floor where a leak shows up. If a supply line or drain under the sink is leaking, close the shutoff valves under the sink, empty the cabinet and call a plumber for the repair while we handle the water.

A kitchen holds a lot of materials that absorb water, such as particleboard cabinet bases, laminate counters and layered flooring. If an appliance caused the leak, the appliance leak page covers the failure, and if the water ran through the floor below, the basement page explains the lower level.

What water does to a kitchen

Particleboard cabinet bases swell and crumble when wet, which is why a leak under the sink can ruin the cabinet even if it dries. Solid wood and plywood cabinets often survive, and the crew tells you which type you have by testing.

Water also runs behind cabinets and under the floor covering, where it can sit for weeks. A moisture meter and thermal camera show the wet area, and the crew opens only what they must.

Common kitchen water sources

  • Sink supply lines and drain traps that loosen or crack.
  • Dishwasher supply, drain and door seals.
  • Refrigerator ice maker lines.
  • Faucet and disposal leaks that drip onto the cabinet base.

Signs of hidden kitchen water damage

Swollen or bubbling cabinet bases, a soft floor near the sink or dishwasher, and a musty smell in the cabinet are the usual signs. Peeling laminate and discolored toe kicks show water has been there a while.

In older Mount Vernon homes, kitchens may have been remodeled in layers, with new flooring over old, which can trap moisture between them. A crew checks each layer so nothing stays wet.

Drying and repair options

The crew extracts surface water, removes ruined cabinet bases and wet flooring, and dries the subfloor and wall bottoms with air movers and dehumidifiers. Cabinets that dry can sometimes stay, and swollen particleboard is replaced.

After drying, a contractor rebuilds. If the leak reached walls, the wall and drywall page covers that repair.

Protecting the kitchen after repair

Replace old braided supply lines, inspect the dishwasher seal, and consider a leak sensor under the sink. A drip tray under the cabinet gives you a warning before a slow leak ruins the base.

The cabinet and toe kick test

Open the cabinet under the sink and look at the floor of it. Swollen particleboard, lifting laminate and dark stains tell you water has been there. Remove the toe kick at the bottom of the cabinet run and look behind it, because water often sits there unseen.

Plywood and solid wood cabinets tolerate a short soaking and can be dried. Particleboard and medium-density fiberboard absorb water like a sponge and rarely recover once swollen.

Sink, countertop and plumbing during repair

To dry the cabinet base and floor, the crew may need the sink and plumbing disconnected, and a plumber should do that and reconnect it. Countertops usually stay in place unless water got under them or the cabinet base has to be removed.

Plan the order of work with the plumber, the restoration crew and the cabinet or flooring contractor, so each trade works once and nothing is sealed over a wet surface.

What affects the cost of kitchen water damage work

Kitchen jobs are small in size but dense in materials.

  • Cabinet material. Particleboard is replaced, while plywood and solid wood can sometimes be dried.
  • Flooring layers. Layered flooring means more to lift and dry.
  • How long it leaked. A slow leak spreads farther than a sudden one found quickly.
  • Extent of cabinet removal. Removing cabinets to reach the wall adds labor.
  • Finish work. Cabinets, counters and flooring are rebuilt after drying.

Kitchen leaks and insurance

Sudden kitchen leaks are often covered, while slow leaks over time often are not. Ask your insurer how your policy treats a long-running leak, and photograph the cabinet before removal.

Questions about kitchen water damage in Mount Vernon

Can swollen cabinets be saved?

Solid wood and plywood often can, and particleboard rarely can. The crew checks the material and shows you the readings.

Do I have to remove the countertop?

Usually not. Cabinet bases can often be reached from below or the side, but the crew will say if the top has to come off.

How do I know if the subfloor is wet?

A moisture meter can tell, and a soft floor, a swollen edge or a musty smell are clues.

Can I keep using the kitchen during drying?

Often partly. The crew will say which areas are safe, and you may lose use of the sink and counter space for a few days.
