When basement water is an emergency

A basement is an emergency when water is rising, when it touches the furnace, water heater or electrical panel, or when the floor drain is bringing in anything that is not clear water. Dark or foul water means sewage, and that is a contamination problem covered on the sewage backup page.

Gas appliances and a wet panel in the same room are the most dangerous combination. Leave the room, call the gas or electric utility or 911 if you smell gas or see sparking, and call us after the hazard is handled.

Where basement water comes from

Sump pump failure is the most common cause during heavy rain, because the pump runs hardest when the power may also be out. Seepage through cracks and joints follows when the ground around the foundation is saturated, and a backed-up floor drain follows when storm or sewer lines are overloaded.

Mount Vernon sits in a river valley, and the city runs a wastewater collection system of about 219 miles of lines and a separate stormwater utility. Those systems are built to carry normal flows, but extreme storms can overload any town. A homeowner cannot control that, so the useful step is to test the sump and know where the main shutoff is before the next heavy rain.

What a finished basement needs

Finished basements hold drywall, carpet, framing and insulation that wick water upward. A flood of only two inches can be wet at the base of every wall, and the crew often cuts the drywall a foot or two above the water line to reach wet insulation.

Unfinished basements are simpler. Concrete floors and masonry walls can usually be cleaned and dried in place, though stored boxes, wood and fabric are not recoverable once contaminated water soaks them.

Cleanup and drying steps

  • Extraction of standing water with pumps and portable units.
  • Removal of wet carpet, pad, drywall and insulation that cannot be dried.
  • Placement of air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, with readings logged each visit.
  • Cleaning and treatment of surfaces left in place, and a final moisture check.

Restoration is not waterproofing

This work removes water and dries the structure. It does not fix why water entered, so if the same basement floods in every heavy rain, you will also need a drainage or waterproofing contractor. We can document where water entered, which helps that conversation.

For heavy drying after the first pass, see structural drying and dehumidification.

What affects the cost of basement cleanup

Two basements with the same water can cost very differently. These are the factors.

  • Water category. Clean groundwater and supply water differ from floor drain backups, which involve sanitizing.
  • Finished or unfinished. Finished space has drywall, flooring and insulation that add removal and replacement.
  • Square footage. Larger rooms need more extraction time and more air movers.
  • Drying days. Concrete and below-grade walls dry slowly, so equipment time can be several days.
  • Contents. Moving and sorting stored items adds labor, and damaged items are documented for your insurer.

Basement water and insurance

Coverage for basement water depends on the cause. Water from a sudden pipe or appliance failure is often covered, water from a backed-up sewer or drain may need a specific endorsement, and groundwater or flood damage usually needs flood coverage. Read your policy or ask your insurer which applies.

Questions about basement water damage in Mount Vernon

Do you handle basement water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Mount Vernon?

Yes. Basement floods happen in the middle of the night during storms, and the line is answered 24/7. Call and tell the dispatcher how deep the water is and whether it touches the panel or furnace.

Is a wet basement always a flood?

No. It can be a failed sump pump, a pipe, seepage or a backup, and each has a different cause and a different insurance question. The written scope records the source.

How long before mold starts?

Growth can begin within a couple of days in damp, warm and still air, which is why fast extraction and drying matter. If it has already started, the mold remediation page explains the next step.

Can I keep my belongings?

Hard items such as metal, plastic and sealed glass can usually be cleaned. Cardboard, paper, fabric and upholstered items that soaked in contaminated water are generally not recoverable.
