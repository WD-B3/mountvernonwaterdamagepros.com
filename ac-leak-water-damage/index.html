AC Leak Water Damage in Mount Vernon, OH

Clogged condensate lines, overflowing drain pans and air handlers that dripped into a ceiling or wall.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

AC leak water damage repair in Mount Vernon, OH usually starts with a condensate line that clogged, because the system keeps running and the pan overflows. If you see water at the air handler or a stain below an attic unit, turn the system off at the thermostat, stop further water and call a technician for the line while we handle the wet materials.

A cooling system removes gallons of moisture from the air on a hot day, and that water has to go somewhere. When the drain line clogs, the pan fills and the water runs into insulation, drywall and framing. If the water ran into a ceiling, the ceiling page explains how stains and cavities are handled.

Why air conditioners leak

Condensate drain lines clog with algae and dust, float switches fail, and drain pans rust through. Evaporator coils can also freeze and then drip when they thaw, which looks like a leak from nowhere.

A unit in an attic or closet is the highest risk, because the pan sits above finished ceilings and the leak shows up as a stain below. Basement or crawl space units leak onto floors and framing instead.

The signs of an AC leak

  • A ceiling stain that appears in summer and stops when the system is off.
  • Water in the pan under an attic or closet air handler.
  • A musty smell near vents or the air handler.
  • A wall below a mini-split unit with a brown or rusty streak.

Why this kind of leak lingers

Condensate leaks are slow, and they happen every time the unit runs. A stain can grow for weeks before it is noticed, and the wet insulation or drywall above it can hold water long after the unit stops.

Mount Vernon summers are humid enough to keep the system running hard, which keeps the drain line busy. Because the water is clean at first but sits in a warm space, mold can start in the cavity, so the crew checks the area with a moisture meter before any repair. If growth is already present, see the mold remediation page.

What the repair involves

An HVAC technician clears the line, replaces the pan or float switch and tests the drain. Then the crew removes wet insulation and drywall, dries the cavity and confirms the readings are dry.

Once dry, the ceiling or wall is patched, textured and painted. Painting before the cavity is dry only hides the problem.

Preventing the next AC leak

Have the condensate line cleared before cooling season, install a float switch that shuts the system down when the pan fills, and add a secondary pan under an attic unit. A water sensor near the unit gives early warning.

Mini-splits, window units and furnace coils

Ductless mini-splits drain through a small tube that kinks or clogs, and the water then drips down the wall behind the unit. Window units leak when they are not tilted slightly outward or when the drain hole clogs.

Furnace and air handler coils drain through a pan and line much like a central unit. In every case, the fix is clearing the drain and finding why it backed up.

What affects the cost of AC leak damage

These factors decide how big the water damage job is.

  • How long it dripped. A leak that ran all summer reaches farther than one found in a day.
  • Unit location. An attic unit above a finished ceiling affects more than a basement unit over concrete.
  • Materials. Wet insulation and sagging drywall are replaced, while sound drywall may only need drying.
  • Mold check. If growth is present, the scope changes and containment may be needed.
  • Finish work. Texture and paint after drying are separate from the water work.

AC leaks and insurance

Sudden overflow damage is often treated differently from slow, long-term leaks, which are often excluded. Ask your insurer how HVAC condensate damage is handled under your own policy and note when you first saw the stain.

Questions about ac leak water damage in Mount Vernon

Can I keep running the AC?

Not if the pan is overflowing. Turn the system off at the thermostat until the drain line is cleared, or the leak will continue.

Will the ceiling stain go away?

It will not fade on its own. Once the source is fixed and the cavity is dry, the stain is sealed with a stain-blocking primer or the drywall is replaced.

Is mold likely?

It can be, because the leak keeps the cavity damp in a warm space. The crew checks readings and tells you what they find.

How often should the drain line be cleared?

Many HVAC technicians clear it at the start of each cooling season, and a technician can tell you what is right for your system.
