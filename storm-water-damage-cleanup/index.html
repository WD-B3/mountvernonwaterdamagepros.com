Rain and wind that got through the roof, windows or siding, and the cleanup that follows.

Emergency storm water damage cleanup in Mount Vernon, OH deals with water that entered through the building envelope during a storm, and the 24/7 line is open before, during and after weather. If you can do so safely, stay clear of damaged roofs and downed lines, protect the opening with a tarp or plastic and photograph everything before moving it.

Storm damage is about how water got in, such as a lifted shingle, a broken window or a branch through the roof. A plain roof leak with no storm event belongs with the ceiling page, and rising water from the ground belongs with the flood page.

When storm damage is an emergency

Water coming through a ceiling, a roof opening you can see daylight through, or a window that no longer closes are emergencies. So is any storm damage that touches electrical wiring, since wet wiring is a fire and shock risk.

Do not climb on a damaged roof. Call the utility for downed lines or gas smells, and call us for the water. Temporary protection such as a tarp can often be arranged through a roofer or a board-up service.

How storms let water in

  • Wind lifts or removes shingles, flashing and vent covers.
  • Falling limbs and trees puncture roofs, siding and windows.
  • Hail cracks shingles and skylights that leak later.
  • Wind-driven rain forces water around windows, doors and chimneys.
  • Gutters and downspouts overflow and push water against walls and foundations.

Storm water and the Kokosing valley

Mount Vernon sits in a river valley with a lot of mature trees, which means limbs on roofs are a regular storm hazard. The city runs a stormwater utility to maintain public drainage, but heavy rain can overwhelm any system, so check that your downspouts discharge away from the foundation and your gutters are clear before storm season.

After a storm, look for water in the attic, damp insulation and stains on upstairs ceilings, because water that entered through the roof often stays hidden for days. A moisture meter and thermal camera confirm what is wet.

What cleanup involves

The crew extracts water, removes wet insulation and drywall that cannot be saved, and dries the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. They document the damage with photos and readings for the insurance claim.

Roof and exterior repair is a separate trade, and a roofer or contractor handles it. A written scope and dated photos help your adjuster see the connection between the storm and the interior damage.

Document before you clean up

Take photos and video of the damage from several angles before moving anything. List damaged items, note the date and time of the storm and keep receipts for emergency repairs such as tarps. Ask your insurer how it handles storm claims, and about any deductible that applies.

What affects the cost of storm water damage work

Storm jobs vary with how much water entered and how long it sat.

  • Entry point. A single roof opening differs from a window and wall opening.
  • Time before response. Water that sat in insulation for days costs more than water found quickly.
  • Materials. Insulation, drywall and flooring are replaced if too wet.
  • Drying time. Wet attics and exterior walls dry slowly.
  • Exterior repair. Roofing and siding are billed by the exterior contractor.

Storm damage and insurance

Storm damage is often covered by homeowners policies, but wind, hail and water have different rules, and some policies have separate deductibles. Read your policy or ask your insurer, and note the date of the storm.

Questions about storm water damage in Mount Vernon

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Mount Vernon?

Yes. Storms do not wait for daylight, and the line is answered 24/7, including during and after severe weather. Tell the dispatcher where water is coming in and whether anyone is hurt.

Can you tarp my roof?

We focus on water removal and drying. A roofer or board-up service handles tarping, and we can help you document the damage.

How soon should I call after a storm?

As soon as it is safe. Water in attic insulation and wall cavities keeps spreading.

Will my insurance cover wind-driven rain?

It depends on the policy and on how the water entered. Ask your insurer, and keep photos of the opening.
