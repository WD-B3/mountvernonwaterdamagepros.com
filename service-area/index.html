Water Damage Service Area in Knox County, OH

Mount Vernon and Gambier, the Kokosing River valley and the neighborhoods in between.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

Where we work

Our dispatch is based in Mount Vernon, ZIP 43050, and our nearby coverage includes Gambier, ZIP 43022, a short drive east. Both sit in Knox County and along the Kokosing River, which is why flooding, drainage and groundwater come up so often in local water damage calls.

The Gambier page covers that village in detail, including its water supply and the buildings around Kenyon College. For everything inside Mount Vernon, the main home page and the services index are the best starting points.

Neighborhoods and landmarks we work around in Mount Vernon

The East High Street Historic District and the downtown blocks around the Woodward Opera House hold some of the oldest buildings in town. Older construction often means plaster walls, original plumbing and basements, so a leak can travel farther than it looks. For those buildings, a moisture map before any repair is not optional.

Mount Vernon Nazarene University and the U.S. Route 36 corridor mix institutional, retail and residential buildings. A commercial loss in a shared building involves neighbors and landlords, which the commercial water damage page explains.

Riverside Park, along the Kokosing River, is where the city draws part of its groundwater supply from wells, according to the city water quality report. For homeowners, the practical tie-back is that the valley has both clean groundwater and a river that can rise in a storm, so know your flood exposure by checking the FEMA flood map for your address.

Water, sewer and storm drainage: who does what

The City of Mount Vernon runs its own water treatment plant, a wastewater system with roughly 219 miles of collection lines and a stormwater utility. The village of Gambier buys its drinking water from the city and maintains its own distribution lines.

That matters when something fails. A leak inside your house is yours and ours to handle. A leak in the street, or a backup that seems to come from the main, belongs to the utility. If you are not sure, call us, and we will tell you what we see and who to contact.

What to expect when you call from either place

The dispatcher asks where the water is, what it came from, whether anyone is at risk and what has been touched. You get shutoff and safety steps first, and the crew arranges the rest. We do not promise an arrival time, but same-day response is available when crews are available.

Everything is explained in plain terms, with scope and price covered before work starts. For a first look at what a specific problem involves, start with the services page or call the line.

Getting ready before you call

  • Know the street address and the nearest cross street.
  • Say whether the water is clean, gray or from a sewer line, if you can tell.
  • Note how long water has been present and which rooms and floors it reached.
  • Have your insurance company name and policy number nearby.
  • Take photos from a safe spot before anything is moved.

Clear answers on that list let the dispatcher plan equipment and tell you the right shutoff steps in the first minutes.

Why the location matters for cleanup

A house near the Kokosing River faces different risks from a house on higher ground. Low lots are more likely to see groundwater in the basement or crawl space after long rain, while higher lots see more roof, plumbing and appliance leaks. The service pages for basements and crawl spaces explain how each is handled.

In Gambier, many buildings are older and some are institutional or multi-unit, which affects the scope. The Gambier page covers what to expect there, and the commercial page covers shops, offices and larger buildings.

Common questions

Do you serve areas outside Mount Vernon and Gambier?

Calls from outside those two places reach the same line. We will tell you honestly whether a crew can reach you or whether a closer company is the better choice.

Who do I call if the leak is in the street?

The utility that owns the line, which for Mount Vernon is the city and for Gambier is the village. For damage inside your home, call us.

Is Gambier covered by the same line?

Yes. The same 24/7 number answers for Mount Vernon and Gambier.
