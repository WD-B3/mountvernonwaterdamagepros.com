Water Damage Restoration in East High Street, Mount Vernon, OH

Water removal and careful drying for homes in the East High Street Historic District.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

The East High Street Historic District is a residential and historic area in Mount Vernon, and it was added to the National Register of Historic Places in 1987. East Gambier Street has its own separate listing from 1976.

Homes with original wood floors, plaster walls and detailed trim need a careful drying plan. This page explains that plan and links to the hardwood floor and wall and drywall pages.

What the listing covers

The National Register record describes the East High Street Historic District as roughly bounded by East Chestnut Street, South Catherine Street, East Vine Street and South Gay Street. It lists 75 contributing buildings and 4 sites.

The separate East Gambier Street District covers addresses 100 to 519 East Gambier Street and lists 20 contributing buildings. The two listings are distinct, and we do not claim a shared boundary or a distance between them.

What we do not claim

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified here, and we found no documented flood history for these blocks. A home can still have water damage from a roof, a pipe, a water heater or an appliance, and those are the cases this page is about.

We also do not claim that older homes get more water damage. We only say that the materials in a historic house, such as plaster, solid wood and old finishes, call for careful handling when they get wet.

Wood floors, plaster and trim

Solid wood floors can cup or crown when they absorb moisture, and they are often dried in place with controlled air movement and low humidity. Rushing can crack boards, so readings guide the pace.

Plaster behaves differently from drywall. It can hold water in the wall and may need a small inspection opening and slow drying. Painted trim is usually removed carefully and reinstalled, so ask the crew to label it.

Lead and older finishes

Paint in older homes may contain lead, and some old building materials may contain asbestos. Do not sand, scrape or tear out suspected materials yourself. Tell the crew the age of the house, and ask whether testing is needed before demolition.

Typical first steps

  • Stop the water at the source if it is safe to do so.
  • Keep people and pets out of areas with standing water or wet outlets.
  • Photograph the damage from a dry spot.
  • Lift small valuables and rugs onto a dry surface.
  • Call the dispatch line and describe the materials that got wet.

Do not use a household vacuum on standing water, and do not operate power equipment while standing in water.

Review and records

Mount Vernon's zoning code includes a Historic Overlay District. Ask the city whether your address is in it before you plan repairs that change the outside of the house. Keep photos, readings and the written scope, because they help with your insurer and with any later sale.

Questions about water damage in East High Street Historic District

Is my home in the East High Street Historic District?

The listing is roughly bounded by East Chestnut, South Catherine, East Vine and South Gay Streets. For a specific address, ask the city or check the National Register record.

Can a historic wood floor be saved after a leak?

Often it can if drying starts quickly and readings are watched, but it is not guaranteed. A crew can tell you after checking the boards and the subfloor.

Will drying damage plaster walls?

Slow, controlled drying reduces the risk, and the crew may open a small area to check the cavity. Ask how they will protect the finishes.

Do I need approval to repair a historic home?

It depends on what changes. Ask the city about the Historic Overlay District before exterior changes. Drying alone usually does not alter the outside.
